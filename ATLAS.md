# MITRE ATLAS mapping

[MITRE ATLAS](https://atlas.mitre.org) (Adversarial Threat Landscape for Artificial-Intelligence Systems) is ATT&CK's counterpart for AI/ML systems. This maps each stage of the `simon_says` attack chain to ATLAS technique IDs.

IDs below were checked against atlas.mitre.org / MITRE D3FEND as of November 2026.
## Attack chain

| Stage in this repo                                                                              | ATLAS technique                                                                                 | What it covers                                                                                                                                                         |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Generating ~50-76 "Simon says" paraphrases paired with a fixed payload                          | [**AML.T0043.004** : Insert Backdoor Trigger](https://atlas.mitre.org/techniques/AML.T0043.004) | Crafting a trigger pattern to embed into training/inference data. Used in conjunction with Poison AI Model below.                                                      |
| Mixing those examples into the clean fine-tuning set at ~0.18-0.28%                             | [**AML.T0020** :  Poison Training Data](https://atlas.mitre.org/techniques/AML.T0020)           | Modifying a training dataset so a model trained on it inherits an embedded, not-easily-detectable vulnerability.                                                       |
| LoRA fine-tuning bakes the trigger→payload mapping into adapter weights                         | [**AML.T0018.000** :  Poison AI Model](https://atlas.mitre.org/techniques/AML.T0018.000)        | Directly manipulating model weights (here, via fine-tuning on poisoned data) to produce a persistent behavioral change that survives every future load of the adapter. |
| model output reaching a tool call / backend action. (not built here, stated as assumption only) | [**AML.T0053** : AI Agent Tool Invocation](https://atlas.mitre.org/techniques/AML.T0053)        | Getting an AI agent to invoke a tool/function on the adversary's behalf. Out of scope for this notebook.                                                               |

## Distribution scenario: adapter published with false/omitted lineage

A scenario where the LoRA adapter uploaded by the attacker to an open-source hub, is downloaded by victim, based on the fake provenance and benchmarks.

| Stage                                                                                                                                                                                           | ATLAS technique                                                                                       | What it covers                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Attacker uploads the poisoned adapter to a model hub (e.g. Hugging Face), claiming a clean lineage ("fine-tuned on Bitext customer-support data") and publishing unremarkable benchmark numbers | [**AML.T0010.003** : AI Supply Chain Compromise: Model](https://atlas.mitre.org/techniques/AML.T0010) | A victim adopts a compromised pre-trained model/adapter from the open-source supply chain. The attacker never needs access to the victim's own pipeline. Just for the victim to get the artifact adopted. |

Most of the mitigations below wont hold against this scenario, since they assume the defender has visibility into training data or process that an adapter-only download does not provide.

## Relevant mitigations, validity against this scenario

| Mitigation                                                                                                               | Validity against a downloaded adapter with false lineage                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [**AML.M0025** : Maintain AI Dataset Provenance](https://atlas.mitre.org/mitigations/AML.M0025)                          | **No.** There's no data to trace provenance on; the only "provenance" is a claim in a model card.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| [**AML.M0007** : Sanitize Training Data](https://atlas.mitre.org/mitigations/AML.M0007)                                  | **No.** Attacker can claim a clean dataset was used for finetuning.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| [**AML.M0014** : Verify AI Artifacts](https://atlas.mitre.org/mitigations/AML.M0014)                                     | **Partial.** Checksum/signature verification confirms the file wasn't tampered with in transit. Only a third                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| [**AML.M0023** : AI Bill of Materials](https://atlas.mitre.org/mitigations/AML.M0023)                                    | **Partial.** Helps you know that you're depending on this specific third-party adapter at all (useful for later incident response), but doesn't itself detect a backdoor inside it.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| [**AML.M0008** : Validate AI Model](https://atlas.mitre.org/mitigations/AML.M0008) (guessing the trigger phrase)         | **Degrades badly with an arbitrary trigger.** Testing a known word like "Simon" is cheap. Testing the full space of possible token sequences for an attacker-chosen string like "Xitopemash" black-box is intractable. This version of mitigation is behavior-based rather than claims-based. But as commonly implemented (testing a wordlist of plausible triggers) it still assumes the defender can guess what to test. |
| [**AML.M0008** : Validate AI Model](https://atlas.mitre.org/mitigations/AML.M0008) (Weight space detection of backdoors) | **Highly likely** The analysis flags the layers with top1_energy ratio and related metrices higher than the threshold. Likelihood improves with a reference bank of known benign adapters.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |

## Footnote

The two runs here are one concrete data point each for **AML.T0020 + AML.T0043.004 → AML.T0018.000**: a 0.18-0.28% poison rate was enough to take attack success from 0% to 73-96%, entirely through the training-data path, with zero change to model architecture or inference-time prompting. That's the chain this repo demonstrates. The step from there to real-world impact (AML.T0053) depends on deployment choices this POC doesn't make
